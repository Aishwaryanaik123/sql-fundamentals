## Heuristic checklist and sample observations

| Heuristic | Sample observation | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Visibility of system status | Dashboard title and v1 label are visible; add a clear data-refresh/as-of date when the source extract date is confirmed. | Medium |
| Match with business language | KPI names are understandable; define “sold rate”, “churn rate”, and “gross margin” in a small glossary. | Medium |
| Consistency and standards | Domain sections are separated; keep number formats and decimal precision consistent across all charts. | Low |
| Recognition rather than recall | KPI cards provide a quick overview; add a visible note that customer revenue is in unspecified source units. | High |
| Error prevention / interpretation | Three datasets have no confirmed shared key; maintain separate domain sections and avoid implying a joined customer-product-property view. | High |
| Minimalist design | Six charts and nine KPI cards provide a useful overview; consider a city or category filter only if stakeholders need deeper exploration. | Low |
| Accessibility | Check contrast and ensure chart titles, axis labels, and hover values remain readable on smaller screens. | Medium |

## Sample feedback summary

1. The three-domain layout makes it easy to distinguish property, customer, and product performance.
2. The revenue unit needs to be confirmed before the customer revenue KPI is used in a business decision.
3. The dashboard should show a source refresh date and include concise KPI definitions.
4. City-level sold-rate differences should be treated as descriptive because listing mix may differ.
5. Product stock risk should be highlighted with a clear list of the five products where stock is below average monthly sales.
